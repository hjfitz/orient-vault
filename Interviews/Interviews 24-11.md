## Questions
### 1. “Walk me through the last thing you personally built end-to-end.”

**Why it works:**  
People who _actually_ deliver describe specifics:
- trade-offs
- constraints
- code they wrote
- where they got stuck
- how they unblocked

People who _pretend_ to lead or float at the edges will describe:
- meetings
- alignment
- ownership
- “we did X”

**Follow-up:**

> “Which part of that work would not have happened without _you_ specifically?”

This forces them to take real ownership.

---

### 2. “Tell me about a time you pushed a piece of work forward when others were stuck.”

**Why it works:**  
You’re filtering for “driver energy”.  
Watch for:
- pairing
- cutting scope
- writing the difficult parts
- teaching while delivering
- unblocking others
- doing the grunt work without ego

Red flags:
- “I escalated to the PM”
- “I facilitated a session”
- “I delegated tasks”

You want **action**, not admin.

---

### 3. “Describe a PR you submitted recently that you were proud of.”

**Why it works:**  
Doers can recall code in detail.  
Pretenders can’t.

Good answers include specifics:

- file structure
- patterns used
- why they chose X over Y
- how they made it readable for teammates
- where they anticipated questions

If they get vague? They don’t code much.

---

### 4. “How do you decide when to go fast versus when to go deep?”

**Why it works:**  
This assesses the exact gap your team has.

You want someone who understands:
- pragmatic delivery
- when to cut corners safely
- when correctness matters
- communication around trade-offs

Red flag answers sound like:
- “I always do my best and it takes the time it takes.”  
    (translation: slow)

---

### 5. “When a teammate is struggling, what do you typically _do_?”

Don’t let them waffle.  
Filter for concrete behaviours:

- pair
- slice the work
- build scaffolding
- write a PoC
- review early
- ask them to talk through their approach

You’re hiring someone who raises the floor _without_ drama.

---

### 6. “Tell me about a time you changed someone else’s mind through technical reasoning.”

**Why it works:**  
This checks:

- influence without authority
- clarity of thought
- technical grounding
- ability to break down trade-offs

You’re testing for the multiplier skill, not the “I led a team” fluff.

---

### 7. “What’s a recent example of code you left better than you found it?”

Good candidates talk about:
- refactoring
- renaming
- extracting functions
- improving test coverage
- reducing complexity
- introducing a small pattern

Bad candidates talk about:
- “I improved our OKRs.”
- “I influenced culture.”

Nope. You want hands-on.

---

### 8. “In your current team, what type of developer do you naturally pair best with?”

This tells you how they’ll fit alongside:
- strong-but-slow
- wants-to-lead-but-not-technical

Good answers:
- “People who need unblocking — I like getting momentum going.”
- “Deep thinkers — I complement them by getting the first 80% done quickly.”

Red flags:
- “I prefer working solo.”
- “I tend to lead the planning and let others execute.”
