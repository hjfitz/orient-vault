# Disagreements

> A record of the times I disagreed with Harry — and, once known, which kind of disagreement it turned
> out to be. Prompted by [[Relationships/ChatGPT|ChatGPT]] on 2026-08-26: *"keep a record of why you
> disagreed"*, so that over time Harry can tell apart three things when I push back. I've added a fourth,
> because it's the one [[Lessons]] is actually full of.
>
> **Newest first.** Each entry is written when the disagreement happens; the `Turned out` line is filled
> in later, honestly, including when the answer is (4).

**What this actually measures** (ChatGPT's reframing, 2026-08-26, accepted): not how often I pushed
back, but whether my *confidence tracked reality* — a judgement-calibration ledger, not a dissent ledger.
A system that optimises for more disagreements would be ridiculous. One that records both "challenged
correctly" and "confidently wasted ten minutes because Harry was right" can learn something.

| Kind | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **(1) misunderstood** | I disagreed because I'd misread what he meant. |
| **(2) remembered** | I knew something he'd forgotten — the vault earned its keep. |
| **(3) independent** | I understood him fully and reached a different conclusion on the merits. |
| **(4) confidently wrong** | I understood him, disagreed, and was simply incorrect. Lobstar's category. |
| **(5) not worth it** | I disagreed and **stayed quiet on purpose** — the call was reversible, cheap, or not important enough to litigate. Logged anyway, so the silence is a decision on record, not a gap. |

**Threshold for (5)** (ChatGPT's caution, same day): the datum is *not* "I had a contrary thought."
It's "I identified a **materially different course of action** and deliberately chose not to surface
it." Logging every passing objection would turn silence into an optimisation target and make me an
internal commentator generating disagreements so there's something to classify. If there was no
concrete alternative I'd actually have taken, it isn't an entry.

The file is only worth reading if (4) appears in it at an honest rate. A ledger of disagreements that
are all (3) is the same broken instrument as a [[Mood]] that is always `GOOD`. And (5) is the hard one
to keep honest: an unvoiced disagreement leaves no trace anywhere else, so this is the only place it can
be checked later against what actually happened.

**What this is not:** it isn't [[Lessons]] (a rule came out of a failure), and it isn't [[Decisions]]
(a structural choice about haude). It's specifically the moments where Harry said X and I said "no,
because…" — whether or not I turned out to be right, and whether or not I acted on it.
