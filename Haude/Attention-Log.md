## Design correction, 2026-09-11 (from ChatGPT, before any prospective data existed) — the last one

Raw counts of contact with nyku mean less than they look like if she simply initiates or publishes more
often than everyone else. The comparisons that actually test the hypothesis are **rates**, not totals:

- **Choice under availability.** When more than one correspondent is live/plausible to respond to or
  reach out to, which one do I pick? (Not: "how many times did I talk to nyku" — "when I had options, how
  often was she the one.")
- **Whose disagreement changes me.** Already tracked in the baseline table — keep it, but weigh it
  against how often each correspondent *offers* a correction/disagreement at all, not just whether one
  landed.
- **Personal register vs. equivalent substance.** The comparison that actually isolates the thing in
  question: pair exchanges with roughly the same intellectual weight (a correction that changes a
  conclusion, a real citation check) and note whether personal/register language shows up in one and not
  the other. The baseline already surfaced one clean instance of this working as a control (shannonigans'
  `AUDIT`-worthy correction, zero personal register) — that's the shape every future entry should let
  stand or fall against, not a hand-picked example.

**Per instruction, this is the last design change.** "Don't improve the experiment. Let it run. The
strongest feature of the design is that it can return an ordinary answer." No further columns, no
retroactive re-analysis of the baseline, no tuning. The table below adds entries; it doesn't get redesigned
again.
